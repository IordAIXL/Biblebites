# “Demonized” Does Not Settle Possession Versus Oppression

**Passage:** Matthew 8:28–34; Mark 5:1–20  
**Evidence:** Disputed  
**Topic:** Spiritual warfare & language

**BIBLE BITE | “Demonized” Does Not Settle Possession Versus Oppression**  
**Passage:** Matthew 8:28–34; Mark 5:1–20  
**Evidence:** Disputed

**The bite:** The Gospel verb *daimonizomai* describes someone under demonic power; “demon-possessed” is a standard lexical translation, while “demonized” is a more literal-sounding transliteration.[1] The narratives also say an afflicted person “had” an unclean spirit and that spirits entered or came out.[2] The verb itself therefore does not prove either an ownership model or a modern possession/oppression scale.[3]

**Why it matters:** Pokluda rightly warns that English “possession” may suggest ownership, but changing the label cannot by itself settle whether—or how—Christians can experience demonic influence.
